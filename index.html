01 / ABOUT

Physics first.
Evidence always.

Different flows.
The same curiosity.

How does fluid motion change the world around it? That question connects my work on wind-turbine aerodynamics, multiphase flow and coastal scour.

I am a CFD researcher at HR Wallingford and a PhD candidate at The Open University. My current work examines how waves and currents move sediment around marine structures, using OpenFOAM simulations and comparisons with laboratory measurements.

Before working in coastal engineering, I studied vortex generators for wind-turbine blades at the Polish Academy of Sciences, investigated turbulent building wakes, and combined CFD with laser Doppler velocimetry at Kamstrup in Denmark.

02 / RESEARCH

Where flow meets a moving bed.

Coastal processes, understood
through multiphase modelling.

DOCTORAL RESEARCH · IN PROGRESS

Multiphase Numerical Modelling of Scour Processes in Wave-Current Environments

The Open University
Affiliated Research Centre: HR Wallingford

I study how interacting air, water and sand shape local scour around monopiles. My work combines Eulerian multiphase CFD with laboratory comparisons to examine the relationship between near-bed flow, potential sediment mobility and bed evolution.

The focus goes beyond maximum scour depth: where erosion and deposition occur, how flow structures develop, and how the bed responds to changing forcing. Supporting studies examine cofferdams under steady currents and structures in transient, tsunami-like flows.
